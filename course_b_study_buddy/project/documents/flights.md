# The Kestrel Project — Flight Log

## Flight 1 — 11 May 2019
First launch. Reached 18,400 m before the balloon burst early, most likely due to a small
tear during filling. Payload recovered 22 km downwind, intact. No usable photographs; the
camera lens fogged during ascent.

## Flight 2 — 28 September 2019
Reached 29,100 m. The camera was triggered by the flight computer and the trigger failed
nine minutes into the flight. No photographs. This flight is the reason the flight computer
no longer controls anything.

## Flight 3 — 15 February 2020
Reached 31,700 m. First successful photographs of the Earth's curvature. Data logging
stopped at 24,000 m when the Arduino Uno froze in the cold. Led directly to the move to the
Raspberry Pi Pico.

## Flight 4 — 6 June 2020
Reached 30,200 m. The single tracker failed on descent and the payload was not found for
eleven days, eventually located by a farmer. This flight is why every flight now carries two
independent trackers.

## Flight 5 — 19 September 2020
Reached 33,100 m — the programme record until Flight 8. The flight computer browned out at
burst because it shared a battery with the cameras. Partial data only.

## Flight 6 — 13 March 2021
Reached 28,800 m. Landed in deep snow. Recovery took nine hours and led to the orange
survival tape now used on every payload.

## Flight 7 — 2 October 2021
Reached 30,900 m. Recorded the programme's coldest temperature, -58 °C at 21,000 m.
Carried the first student experiment: a yeast viability test proposed by a first-year student.

## Flight 8 — 21 May 2022
Reached **34,600 m**, the current programme record. Full data, full photographs, recovered
in 90 minutes. Widely considered the programme's most successful flight.

## Flight 9 — 8 October 2022
Reached 29,400 m. Landed in a steep valley with no LoRa reception. Recovered only because of
the satellite tracker.

## Flight 10 — 25 March 2023
Reached 32,200 m. Carried three student experiments, the most of any flight. All three
returned usable data.
