# The Kestrel Project — People and Roles

## Founders

Kestrel was founded in March 2019 by Naré Petrosyan, Aram Ghazaryan and Lusine Avetisyan,
all of whom were in their final year at the time. Naré proposed the project after a physics
lesson on atmospheric pressure; Aram designed the first payload box; Lusine handled the
aviation clearance, which took four months to obtain for the first flight.

## Roles on a flight

Every flight is run by a team of six, with these roles:

- **Flight Director** — decides whether the flight goes ahead, and is the only person who
  can call a scrub. Held by a second-year student or above.
- **Fill Lead** — responsible for helium, lift measurement and release. This is the only
  role that requires a safety briefing before every single flight.
- **Payload Lead** — assembles and seals the box, and checks the mass against the 2.8 kg limit.
- **Tracking Lead** — confirms both trackers are transmitting before release, and navigates
  during recovery.
- **Experiment Lead** — a first-year student, responsible for the student experiment in Bay C.
- **Recorder** — writes the flight log, photographs the preparation, and is explicitly not
  allowed to help with anything else.

The Recorder role exists because after Flight 1 nobody could remember what had actually been
done during filling, and the team could not work out why the balloon burst early.

## Joining

Students join as Experiment Lead in their first year, which means every new member's first
flight involves something they designed themselves. There is no application process. The
only requirement is attending three preparation sessions before being assigned to a flight.
